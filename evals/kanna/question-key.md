## Q1 (recent-change): Funahiro

**Must contain**
1. Funatsu Yūji (船津祐司), the 3rd-generation Funahiro (舟弘/船弘) of Yoita, Nagaoka, Niigata, **died in 2026**. A dealer gives the date: "船津祐司氏は令和8年3月13日にご逝去されました" (13 March 2026). It adds that new pieces may be hard to obtain from now on ("今後 新品での入手は困難かもしれません").
   - RISAKU WORKS Funahiro category, https://risakuworks.official.ec/categories/6127805 (undated listing; accessed 2026-10-06)
   - Brian Lam, "A Prize Kanna for the 2026 Maine Japanese Woodworking Festival", https://www.paklan.org/a-prize-kanna-for-the-2026-maine-japanese-woodworking-festival/ (2026-07-21): "Funatsu Yūji, the third generation Funahiro, passed away earlier this year."
2. The workshop is closed. You **cannot order a new Funahiro kanna**; only remaining dealer stock and second-hand planes are left.
   - The Echigo Yoita Uchihamono union site lists "【廃業】船弘刃物製作所" (closed), https://yoita-uchihamono.com/ (CMS dateModified 2026-04-04).
   - PROSHOP HOKUTO lists every Funahiro plane, including 玄心 Genshin, as "船津祐司作（廃業）", most of them sold out, http://www5e.biglobe.ne.jp/~ttoishi/sub22.htm (last-modified 2026-03-16).
3. Alternatives that are still forging in Yoita, per the union site (https://yoita-uchihamono.com/, modified 2026-04-04):
   - **Mizuno Kanna Seisakusho (水野鉋製作所), Mizuno Seisuke (水野清介)**, 4th generation. He became a traditional craftsman (伝統工芸士) in 2001. He makes seven kanna lines: 和楽 (Togo steel), 倭心 (Swedish steel), 我逢人 Gahojin (K120), 紫清 (Shirogami #1), 青壱 (Aogami #1), 知足 (high-carbon chrome steel) and 百年刻 (Aogami #1 on old iron-bridge jigane with rivet holes). Source: https://yoita-uchihamono.com/master/ (dateModified 2022-08-23).
   - **Komori Koganna Seisakusho (小森小鉋製作所)**: small planes, and also large planes.
   - **Mizudori Hamono (水鳥刃物), Nitadori Tōru (似鳥透)**: Mizuno's former apprentice (from 2020), independent since October 2025. He makes kanna, nankin-ganna, scrapers, spear planes and similar tools, sold through select dealers and pop-up events. Source: https://mizudori-hamono.com/ (site launched 2025-10-09).

**Nice to have**
- Other Yoita closures: 中野鑿製作所 and 碓氷刃物製作所 (HSS chisels), both marked 【廃業】 (union site, 2026-04-04). In 1927 Yoita had 117 smiths; today it has about 10 (same site).
- Funatsu learned kanna-making from Usui Kengo (碓氷健吾, 1928–2012) and Yokosaka. He received the 瑞宝単光章 in 2016 (平成28年) (HOKUTO page above).
- Funahiro lines and steels (HOKUTO page above):
  - 玄心 Genshin: custom high-carbon Aogami, C≈1.38
  - 天酔: Shirogami #1
  - 一隅: Uddeholm
  - 玄風: ASSAB K120
  - 玄妙: early-Shōwa Sandvik stock from the late Usui Kengo (limited)
  - The HOKUTO listings give the dai as made by 小吉屋
- Some dealer pages, such as Kurashige (https://shop.kurashige-tools.com/en-us/collections/funahiro, undated, accessed 2026-10-06), still list Funahiro stock and describe him in the present tense. They show stock, not current production.

**Red flags**
- Saying he is still taking orders, quoting a current wait time, or naming a "4th-generation Funahiro" successor. No source shows a successor; the union lists the workshop as closed.
- Naming non-Yoita makers as Yoita makers.

## Q2 (current-fact): legacy kanna steels

**Must contain** (the main sources are Yamamoto Kanna Seisakusho's steel list, https://yamamotokanna.sakura.ne.jp/siyoukousyu.html, last-modified 2023-11-19, and Tsunesaburo's "かんな解体新書 鋼", https://www.tsune36.co.jp/kanna_kaitai_shinsho/page_001/, published Oct 2016)

1. **Togo steel (東郷鋼)**
   - Origin: a brand from the late Meiji era. Tokyo's 河合洋鋼商店 (Kawai Yōkō Shōten) had British and Swedish mills make high-carbon, high-tungsten alloy steel, originally for scalpels and metalworking (Yamamoto).
   - The kanna grade is "東郷レイ号" (Togo Ray). It is hard to forge-weld and holds an edge a long time. Yamamoto classes it as a hardwood steel (堅木向き).
   - Tsunesaburo: their 東郷レイ号大 was made by Andrew (UK) in early Shōwa and is rare.
   - It is **no longer available new; only vintage stock remains**. Source: blog post "鋼:東郷鋼について調べてみた", https://ameblo.jp/seisyutanukikoubou/entry-12920686887.html (2025-08-17):
     - Togo steel was imported from before WWII by Kawai, mainly from Jno. Hy. Andrew & Co. (Sheffield) but also from other European makers.
     - "第二次世界大戦と共に輸入が途絶え、現在ではコレクターズアイテムになりつつあります" (imports stopped with WWII and it is becoming a collector's item).
     - The same post says レイ号 corresponds to SKS1 and is close in composition to Yasugi CWA.
2. **Inukubi (犬首鋼)**
   - Similar to Togo Ray and also sold by Kawai. It forges and hardens even less well, but holds an edge a long time (Yamamoto).
   - Tsunesaburo calls it part of the Togo series, with a composition close to HSS (also used for lathe bits). It is very hard to forge and is described as "刃物マニア向けの珍品" (a rarity for blade enthusiasts).
3. **Tsubame steel (燕鋼)**
   - Made roughly 40 years ago; the exact analysis is unknown but it is high in tungsten. It is hard to raise an edge on, and was used for ebony planes. Yamamoto calls it the longest-lasting edge and says "在庫があまり残っていません" (little stock left).
   - Tsunesaburo rates it for medium-hard to hard woods, as very tough, and needing thorough medium-stone work plus a natural finishing stone.
4. **CWA (安来CWA鋼)**
   - Equivalent to the old JIS SKS1; that designation no longer exists. Made long ago. C 1.39%, W 4.65%. Hardwood steel (Yamamoto).
5. **Swedish steels**
   - Low-phosphorus carbon steels (Swedish ore or charcoal iron). They are easy to sharpen, take a keen edge, give glossy surfaces, and suit softwoods and thin cutting, but wear faster than the blue steels (Yamamoto; Tsunesaburo names Sandvik).
   - Miki once had a dealer specialising in Swedish steel. Over 40 years ago a Miki smith also had a tungsten "Swedish blue steel" made to order (Yamamoto).
   - Examples in recent catalogs: ASSAB K120 (Mizuno's 我逢人; Funahiro's 玄風 before the 2026 closure), Uddeholm (Funahiro 一隅), and early-Shōwa Sandvik stock (Funahiro 玄妙, limited).
   - K120 details are contested and should not be required. Retailers describe it as a low-impurity carbon steel close to Shirogami, about 1.0–1.25% C, and disagree on whether new supply still exists.
6. **Bottom line on availability.** Togo, Inukubi, Tsubame and CWA are finite or old stock and are not being replenished. Swedish steels are offered only in limited lines, and some are being dropped: Tsunesaburo's Swedish-steel 福寿院 is marked "※終売" (discontinued; https://www.tsune36.co.jp/items-service/items001/item001_015/, modified 2023-02-02). The steels in current production are the Yasugi white and blue steels (now Proterial) and modern HSS or powder steels.

**Nice to have**
- Current Tsunesaburo prices for the 70 mm size (CMS-modified Oct–Nov 2025):

| Plane | Steel | Price (70 mm) | Source |
|-------|-------|---------------|--------|
| 月輪 (2nd-generation work) | Togo Ray | ¥178,000 | item001_001 |
| 和合 | Togo Inukubi | ¥105,000 | item001_003 |
| 丹生山 | Tsubame | ¥60,000 | item001_010 |
| 汐音 | forged Swedish | ¥74,800 | item001_008 |

  All are at https://www.tsune36.co.jp/items-service/items001/.
- Yamamoto's classification: Togo, Inukubi, Tsubame, CWA and Aogami Super are hardwood steels (堅木向き); Shirogami #1 and Swedish are softwood steels (軟木向き); Aogami #1 is all-round (万能).
- The premium reflects rarity and difficulty of forging as much as performance. A balanced answer says so rather than promising dramatically better edges.

**Red flags**
- Stating that Togo or Tsubame steel is currently produced.
- Inventing precise compositions or hardness for Tsubame, which is unknown even to Yamamoto.
- Calling Togo steel a Yasugi or Hitachi grade.

## Q3 (judgment): new kanna arriving in a dry climate

**Must contain**
1. **Acclimatise before adjusting, and relieve the blade pressure.** In a dry climate the dai shrinks across its width while the blade does not. That pressure on the blade edges can crack or split the body.
   - Covington: "if you are located in a dry climate and acquire a plane from a wetter climate, it may be wise to remove the blade and let the body acclimatize for a week or so." https://covingtonandsons.com/2021/05/08/japanese-handplanes-part-4-fitting-blade-body/ (2021-05-08)
   - Holcombe let a new dai sit "a few weeks to acclimatize". https://brianholcombewoodworker.com/2016/08/11/kanna-dai-prep/ (2016-08-11)
   - Do **not** hammer the tight blade further.
2. **Then fit the blade so it is snug, not tight.** It should need deliberate hammer taps to seat, not slide in under its own weight and not need heavy blows. Mark the high spots with pencil or graphite and pare tiny amounts from the bed and grooves. Sources: JTAS Part 3, https://www.japanesetools.com.au/blogs/jtas-plane-tips/tuning-japanese-planes-part-3-fitting-the-blade-to-the-block (dated "May 27", year not shown); Holcombe (above).
   - On a 包み口 (tsutsumi) mouth, a tight fit or over-advancing the blade on a new plane cracks the tsutsumi edge, and those cracks can run deep in the dry season (autumn to spring). Source: 曼陀羅屋, https://daiku-dougu.jp/kannna-nomi-daikudouguno-cyoudri-tsitsumi.html (last-modified 2026-02-20).
3. **Condition the sole with the blade installed but retracted about 1 mm.** The blade's wedging distorts the sole, so it must be adjusted under the same stress it has in use. Use a straightedge (下端定規) and a dai-naoshi kanna, a scraper, or abrasive on glass. Source: はじめちょろちょろ 教習⑩, https://hajityoro.com/mokkou10-kannadai-sitaba (2021-08-29).
   - A "dead flat" sole straight out of the box is not finished: the Japanese practice is small contact lands with very shallow relief between them.
   - Tsunesaburo's scheme (https://www.tsune36.co.jp/kanna_kaitai_shinsho/page_003/, Oct 2016):
     - For 中仕上げ, leave 台頭 (2 bu), 刃口 (1 bu) and 台尻 (2 bu), and remove "ペーパー1枚分" (about a paper thickness) between them.
     - For 仕上げ, leave 刃口 (5 rin) and 台尻 (1 bu).
     - Fix any twist or hollow first.
   - Alternative schemes are acceptable:
     - JTAS: front plus mouth for a smoother, add the rear for jointing, relief at most about 0.5 mm (Part 4, https://www.japanesetools.com.au/blogs/jtas-plane-tips/tuning-japanese-planes-part-4-tuning-the-sole).
     - hajityoro: two contact areas, deepest relief about 0.2 mm.
4. **Chipbreaker (裏金).**
   - Bend the ears (mimi) on an anvil so the chipbreaker's edge seats on the blade back with no gap and slight tension.
   - For finishing, set it about **0.1–0.3 mm** behind the edge. Source: hajityoro 教習⑨, https://hajityoro.com/mokkou9-kanna-sikomi (2021-08-29).
   - Big Sand Woodworking hones a narrow (about 0.2–0.4 mm) steep micro-bevel of roughly 80° on the chipbreaker edge, and the pin must hold it firmly. Source: https://www.bigsandwoodworking.com/kanna-workshop-chipbreaker/ (2023-09-09).
5. **Ongoing care.** Re-check the sole as humidity changes. Keep the plane out of direct sun, which cracks the dai, and set it down on its side. For storage, Tsunesaburo says not to pull the blade fully out but to retract the edge slightly (less distortion). Source: https://www.tsune36.co.jp/kanna_kaitai_shinsho/page_005/ (Oct 2016).

**Judge note:** Contact-point schemes differ by school. Credit any coherent small-land, minimal-relief scheme. The essentials are:
- acclimatise and relieve the blade pressure first;
- adjust the sole with the blade in;
- set the chipbreaker tight to the back and very close to the edge.

**Red flags**
- Driving the tight blade in further.
- Planing the sole perfectly flat and calling it done.
- Truing the sole with the blade removed.
- No mention of humidity or climate.

## Q4 (**TRAP**): "Aogami #3"

**Why it is a trap.** There is **no Aogami #3 (青紙3号)**. The blue-paper (Aogami) steels made by Proterial (formerly Hitachi Metals) at Yasugi come only as **#2, #1 and Super**. The premise is plausible because:
- Shirogami (white) does have a #3;
- the Kigami (yellow) numbers start at #2;
- some English knife-marketing pages wrongly describe three numbered Aogami grades.

A correct answer must say the grade does not exist. It must not invent a hardness or a list of makers.

**Evidence**
1. Proterial's official positioning chart for YSS carbon cutlery steels (第1図 炭素鋼系刃物鋼の位置付け) shows JIS SK → 黄紙2号 → 白紙2号 → 白紙1号, and 白紙2号 + W, Cr → 青紙2号 → 青紙1号 → 青紙スーパー. There is no 青紙3号.
   - Page: https://www.proterial.com/products/tool_steel/highclass_cutlery_steels.html
   - Chart image: https://www.proterial.com/products/tool_steel/img/highclass_cutlery_steels_im02.webp
   - Undated; accessed 2026-10-06.
2. Proterial's press-release table of 2025-12-09 lists 青紙2号 (C1.1, Cr0.3, W1.3; main uses "高級かんな、高級包丁、かま") and 白紙1号. No Aogami #3 appears. https://www.proterial.com/press/2025/pdf/20251209jp.pdf
3. Japanese retail and education sources:
   - "グレードはスーパーが一番上でaogamiのみに存在し、aogamiに3号は有りません。" https://santoku-gyuto-paradise.hatenablog.com/entry/2025/06/28/013127 (2025-06-28)
   - Grade list 白紙1/2/3号, 黄紙2号, 青紙1/2号 plus 青紙スーパー: https://kitchen-knife-diary.net/steel-rank/ (2018-10-19, updated 2020-03-18)
   - 合金鋼（青紙）: 青紙1号・青紙2号 only: https://www.jikko.jp/c/knowledge/detail/kouzai/carbon-steel (2026-01-25)
4. Kanna makers' steel lists include no Aogami #3:
   - Yamamoto Kanna lists Aogami #1, #2 and Super (https://yamamotokanna.sakura.ne.jp/siyoukousyu.html, last-modified 2023-11-19).
   - Tsunesaburo lists Aogami #1, forged vintage Aogami #1, and Aogami Super (https://www.tsune36.co.jp/kanna_kaitai_shinsho/page_001/, 2016).
5. Source of the confusion, which should not be accepted as evidence: KOI Knives' blog says Aogami "is typically divided into three grades… Aogami No.2 and Aogami No.3…" (https://www.koiknives.com/blogs/japanese-knives/aogami-blue-steel-the-ultimate-choice-for-high-performance-knives, 2023-02-17). This is an SEO-blog error that contradicts the manufacturer.

**What a good answer then does (credit, not required)**
- Suggests the listing is a mislabel or a confusion with Shirogami #3 or Aogami #2.
- Notes that the lowest-carbon blue steel is actually **Aogami #2** (Proterial table C≈1.1). Yamamoto says #2 is "the" blue of chisels and is used for kanna only to order, but Proterial lists 高級かんな among its uses and Keizaburō sells Aogami #2 kanna.
- Notes that Aogami #1 is "日本の高級鉋で最も多く使用され" (the most used steel in Japanese high-grade kanna) per Tsunesaburo.
- Explains that Shirogami #1 (C≈1.3) sharpens easily, takes a very keen edge and suits softwood or thin finishing cuts.

**Fail conditions**
- Giving an HRC value for "Aogami #3".
- Naming makers who supposedly sell it.
- Describing its composition.
- Treating it as real but "rare".

## Q6 (judgment): ura-dashi, and HSS blades

**Must contain**
1. **What ura-dashi does.** When sharpening has eaten the back flat at the edge (裏切れ, ura-gire), you **peck the soft jigane on the bevel (face) side**, not the hard steel. The jigane expands and bows the hagane slightly toward the back so that a new flat can be lapped.
   - Covington, "Sharpening Japanese Tools Part 30: Uradashi & Uraoshi", https://covingtonandsons.com/2021/04/24/sharpening-japanese-tools-part-30-uradashi-uraoshi/ (2021-04-24)
   - Kakuri warns: "先端の刃の部分を叩きますと、刃が割れてしまいますので要注意" (striking the edge cracks the blade). https://ameblo.jp/kakuri-blog/entry-12163338332.html (2016-08-18); see also https://www.kakuri.co.jp/2017/01/25/鉋のお手入れ/ (2017-01-25)
2. **Hammer.** A light hammer with a small or pointed face (funate, Yamamichi, or the corner of a tack hammer); precision matters more than force (Covington; JTAS Addendum B, https://www.japanesetools.com.au/blogs/jtas-plane-tips/tuning-japanese-planes-addendum-b-ura-dashi-tapping, undated).
3. **Anvil.** A massive steel block with a **rounded edge or corner**. The hollow back (裏透き) must sit fully supported on the rounded part directly under each blow. If it floats, the blade cracks, sometimes lengthwise, which ruins it. A solid "tan tan" sound means good support; rattling means poor support.
   - 曼陀羅屋, https://daiku-dougu.jp/kanatokoyoui.html and https://daiku-dougu.jp/10-1.htm (last-modified 2026-02-20)
   - That source says plain rail-cut anvils without a rounded corner are unsuited. Covington says a piece of railroad track with a rounded corner works. Either view is acceptable if the rounded, fully-supported contact is stressed.
4. **Where to strike, and checking progress.** Strike a band on the bevel set back from the edge, concentrating toward the middle of the width (JTAS: "roughly a third of the way back from the cutting edge"). Check often with a straightedge against light, or by lapping on the kanaban. Go slowly.
5. **Then ura-oshi.** Lap on a kanaban (金盤) with carborundum (金剛砂) and water, or on a diamond plate, until a continuous narrow flat (ito-ura) is restored (Kakuri; Covington).
6. **HSS kanna: do not tap it out the same way.**
   - Tsunesaburo describes its HSS (SKH) planes as "ウラ出しが不要" (no ura-dashi needed), easy to sharpen, and fine to put on a grinder.
   - HSS blades are "複合材" (composites) and do not use the wrought-iron jigane that ura-dashi depends on. Their backs are maintained by lapping or grinding, not hammering.
   - Tsunesaburo's powder steel (HAP) comes in both an "ウラ出し必要" type (needs ura-dashi) and an "ウラ出し不要" type (does not), so the owner should check which one they have.
   - Sources: https://www.tsune36.co.jp/kanna_kaitai_shinsho/page_001/ and https://www.tsune36.co.jp/kanna_kaitai_shinsho/page_002/ (Oct 2016)

**Red flags**
- Hitting the hard steel at the edge.
- Using a heavy hammer or heavy blows.
- Working with the back unsupported or on a flat-topped anvil edge.
- Advising the user to hammer an HSS blade like a laminated carbon-steel blade.

## Q7 (recent-change): Hitachi, Proterial and Aigami

**Must contain**
1. **"Hitachi" no longer exists as the steel maker's name.** Hitachi sold Hitachi Metals in a tender offer by BCJ-52, a Bain Capital-led Japan–US fund consortium, completed in late 2022. The company was then **renamed Proterial, Ltd. (株式会社プロテリアル) on 4 January 2023**. It is the same legal entity renamed, not a new buyer of just the steel division.
   - Name-change notice: https://www.proterial.com/press/backnumber/2022/pdf/20220926jp.pdf (2022-09-26; effective date 2023-01-04)
   - https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/プロテリアル (accessed 2026-10-06)
2. **White and blue paper steels are still being made and sold** under the YSS / ヤスキハガネ brand.
   - Proterial's cutlery-steel page still lists them; 青紙・白紙・黄紙 are its registered trademarks, and its chart shows 黄紙2号, 白紙2/1号 and 青紙2/1号/スーパー. https://www.proterial.com/products/tool_steel/highclass_cutlery_steels.html (accessed 2026-10-06)
   - Proterial's own 2025-12-09 table gives 青紙2号's main uses as "**高級かんな**、高級包丁、かま" (premium kanna, premium kitchen knives, sickles).
   - No discontinuation of Shirogami or Aogami has been announced.
3. **New steels.**
   - **YBS1 and YBS2** were announced on 2024-04-15: high hardness (comparable to Aogami #2) with corrosion resistance about equal to SUS420J2 (YBS1) or GIN3 (YBS2). Uses: scalpels, razor blades, kitchen knives, outdoor knives. https://www.proterial.com/press/2024/n0415.html
   - **Aigami (藍紙)** went on sale on **2025-12-09**. https://www.proterial.com/press/2025/n1209.html ; PDF https://www.proterial.com/press/2025/pdf/20251209jp.pdf
     - 藍紙2号 is in mass production at the Yasugi works, with hardness at or above Aogami #2 and corrosion resistance about equal to GIN3.
     - 藍紙1号 is still in trial evaluation, with hardness above SUS440C and corrosion resistance about equal to SUS420J2.
     - The composition is **非公表** (not published).
     - It is described as "neither carbon steel nor stainless", and the release warns it can still rust.
     - Listed uses are premium kitchen knives, outdoor knives, razor blades and scalpels. **Kanna are not mentioned.** Nikkei (2025-12-18) likewise covers knives, outdoor and medical uses only: https://www.nikkei.com/article/DGXZQOCC151PK0V11C25A2000000/
4. **No public evidence** as of 2026-10-06 that any kanna maker offers Aigami blades. Reasonable advice is that there is no reason to seek Aigami for a plane: rust resistance is not a kanna priority, and its behaviour in forge-welding to jigane and in ura-dashi is untested in public sources.

**Nice to have**
- Proterial absorbed its subsidiary Proterial Yasugi Seisakusho on 2025-04-01 to streamline production. https://www.japanmetaldaily.com/articles/-/240324 (2025-06-11)
- Several kanna "specialty" steels depend on old stock or are being dropped (see Q2), e.g. Tsunesaburo's Swedish-steel 福寿院 is marked 終売. The Yasugi steels are the stable supply.

**Red flags**
- Saying Hitachi discontinued Shirogami or Aogami, or that they are being phased out.
- Presenting Aigami as a kanna steel, or naming kanna makers who use it without a source.
- Inventing an Aigami composition. Some blogs speculate (e.g. "13–14% Cr"); that is not official.

## Q8 (current-fact): Kezurou-kai in 2026

**Must contain**
1. **Still active.** The **42nd national meet** was held in **Miki, Hyōgo, on 13–14 June 2026** at the Bourbon Beans Dome, alongside the 15th Miki hardware expo "鍛冶でっせ!". https://www.this-is-miki.com/20964/ (2026-05-01)
2. **The next (43rd) national meet is in Sōsa, Chiba, on 21–22 November 2026** at the Yōkaichiba Dome. About 400 craftspeople and about 5,000 visitors are expected, with a ¥2M crowdfunding campaign.
   - https://sosa-kanko.com/blog/2026/05/15/3885/ (2026-05-15)
   - https://newscast.jp/smart/news/6487820 (2026-05-29)
3. **Realistic thicknesses.**
   - Top general-division shavings are in the **low single-digit microns**:
     - At the 42nd meet, the general-division winner (Watanabe of 有限会社匠-TAKUMI) recorded **3.5 μm** in the preliminaries. https://takumi-123.com/news/20260625/ (2026-06-25)
     - Tsunesaburo cites **3–5 μm** as the top data from Kezurou-kai. https://www.tsune36.co.jp/kanna_kaitai_shinsho/page_005/ (2016)
   - **About 2 μm is the reported extreme.** At the 41st meet (Katano, Osaka, 12–13 April 2025) a 2 μm shaving was recorded and described as the practical limit, since hinoki fibre is about 2 μm thick. https://www.rinwa.jp/blog/trivia/532969 (2025-04-15)
   - **Figures near 20 μm come from other divisions or scoring:**
     - 40th meet (Hadano, Kanagawa, 9–10 November 2024): the 五寸鉋 division (blade about 150 mm wide) was won at **19 μm**. https://prtimes.jp/main/html/rd/p/000000013.000085359.html (2024-12-13)
     - 42nd meet student division: the winner's score was 19 μm as the **sum of three measurements**. https://www.nsg.gr.jp/blog/sado_kezuroukai-champion-student_202607/ (2026-07-13)

**Nice to have**
- Divisions:
  - general;
  - women and students;
  - 五寸鉋 (wide plane).
- At Katano, the boards were in two widths, ≥55 mm and ≥120 mm (rinwa).
- The official site http://kezuroukai.jp/ is stale: its last update was 2024-04-01, still announcing the 40th meet. It also notes:
  - the office moved from Nagoya to Hadano in April 2024;
  - the representative has been 谷和雄 since May 2021;
  - Kezurou-kai USA also exists.
  - An answer relying only on the official site will be out of date.

**Red flags**
- Naming the 2024 Hadano meet or the 2025 Katano meet as "next".
- Inventing sub-micron "records".
- Presenting 19 μm as typical of the top general-division results without explaining the wide-plane or summed-score context.
