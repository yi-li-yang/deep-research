## Q1
I don't have reliable details on Norra's kit, meaning her ability numbers, cooldowns and standard build. So I can't honestly tell you her exact power spikes, and I'd rather not guess. Here's how to work them out quickly and how to play the lane on Syndra.

**Working out her spikes**
- Read her abilities, either under Collection → Champions in the client or by clicking her in game and hovering her ability icons. Note her main damage/CC spell and its cooldown at rank 1 vs. rank 5, whether she has a dash or self-peel, and what her ult does. If her ult closes the gap or kills from long range, level 6 is when she becomes dangerous. If it's a utility ult, her spikes come mostly from items.
- After each loss, check what she rushed. Finishing that first item is usually her biggest lane spike.
- Riot often adjusts new champions within a patch or two of release, so check the patch notes since 26.18 for changes to her.

**Playing it on Syndra**
- Hold E for when she commits instead of fishing with it. Q-E is both your self-peel and your kill threat.
- Trade when her main spell is on cooldown: step up and Q her every time she walks up to last-hit.
- Shove so you hit 6 first when you can. Until you know what her ult does, respect her level 6 and save Flash for it.
- If she's winning trades, start Doran's Shield and/or take Second Wind. Play for wave control and 2v2s with your jungler instead of forcing trades.
- If her damage is magic burst, early magic resist (e.g. Banshee's Veil) or a Zhonya's to stall her all-in helps a lot.
- If she's in half your games and keeps beating you, ban her until you've played the matchup more or she gets nerfed.
## Q2
I'm not certain what the mid quest gives on the current patch, because quest rewards can be retuned from patch to patch. As I understand the version that launched with the 2026 season, finishing it gave mid laners a tempo reward: a faster, empowered Recall plus a free boots upgrade, to help you reset and roam more efficiently. Treat that as unconfirmed. The in-game tooltip is the authority, so hover the quest tracker to see the current reward and exactly what counts toward progress.

**Finishing it fast** (this applies whatever the exact numbers are):
- Stay in your assigned lane. Quests are tied to your assigned role, so swapping lanes or leaving mid early slows it down.
- Don't lose CS. Aim for 8+ CS/min. Push the wave into their tower before you back or roam, so your minions don't die under your own turret while you're gone.
- Take tower plates whenever you have priority. They're the cheapest extra gold in lane until the plates fall.
- Only roam when it pays off. A roam that gets a takedown or an objective is worth it. One that costs you two waves and gets nothing sets you back.
- Pick and build for waveclear. Champions and first items that push waves fast let you farm safely and move when you choose.
- Don't die. Each death costs you the waves you miss while you're dead and walking back, which is the biggest single delay in lane.
## Q3
Yes, TP is still worth taking in the right situations, just not by default. If most top laners in your games run Ignite, that fits how the top quest works: its reward pays off for winning and farming your lane, which pushes people toward kill pressure. I believe the launch version of the quest also upgraded Teleport to Unleashed Teleport when you finished it. I'm not certain that's still how it works on the current patch, so check the quest tooltip.

**Take Teleport when:**
- You're on a tank, scaling or weak-side pick that won't kill your opponent (Ornn, Sion, K'Sante, Malphite).
- You're losing a ranged or poke matchup and need to back without giving up waves and XP. TP keeps your lane income, and your quest, on track.
- You're a split-pusher (Fiora, Jax, Camille, Tryndamere, Yorick) who wants to hold a side lane and still join drake and Baron fights.

**Take Ignite when:**
- You have kill pressure at levels 2, 3 or 6 (Darius, Renekton, Pantheon, Riven, Olaf, Sett, Irelia) and want to snowball.
- The enemy laner or team relies on healing (Vladimir, Aatrox, Warwick, Dr. Mundo), so Ignite's Grievous Wounds matters.
- You're counterpicking and expect lane priority.

**Something else:**
- Ghost on juggernauts that need to stick to targets (Darius, Garen, Olaf, Udyr, Nasus, Mordekaiser).
- Exhaust when you're the weaker side against hyper-duelists like Fiora, Tryndamere, Yone or Irelia.

Rule of thumb for solo queue: if your champion wins lane, take Ignite or Ghost. If it doesn't, or your value comes from being across the map, take TP. Solo queue teams don't reliably play around a TP flank, so most of TP's value comes from protecting your lane rather than from big plays.

I can't give you a reliable tier list for the current patch. I don't have dependable win-rate data for it, and jungle strength shifts from patch to patch. What I can give you is junglers that have been consistently strong in Emerald solo queue over many patches, and a quick way to check which of them are strong right now.

**Historically reliable in Emerald**
- Carries and snowballers that punish poor team coordination: Nocturne, Warwick, Master Yi, Kayn, Kha'Zix, Briar, Bel'Veth, Shaco.
- Skirmishers with early pressure: Xin Zhao, Jarvan IV, Vi, Graves.

**Checking the current patch (two minutes)**
1. On U.GG, Lolalytics or OP.GG, filter to Emerald+ and the current patch. Shortlist champions at roughly 51.5%+ win rate with a meaningful pick rate (a few percent, not a handful of games).
2. Read the latest patch notes. Junglers that were buffed last patch and haven't been nerfed yet are usually the best LP grabs.
3. Don't chase pro picks. The Worlds patch tends to target whatever dominates pro play, and picks that are only strong in solo queue are less likely to be touched.

**To actually climb:** pick 2–3 from the list that fit how you play (one carry, one skirmisher, one engage/tank for when your team needs it) and play them every game. Focus on clean full clears, early objectives and tracking the enemy jungler. A champion you've played 50 times usually beats this week's top-tier pick that you've played twice.

## Q6
I don't have reliable details on League Classic. I can't confirm exactly what it is, which champions are in it right now, or what it rewards, so I won't guess at a champion list. Here's how to find the answers in a minute, and what's usually true for Riot's alternate game modes:

- **What it is and who you can play:** open the mode's card in the client's Play menu. Riot lists the rules there, and the eligible champions if the mode restricts the pool. The mode's announcement or patch notes will have the same information.
- **Rank:** unless the mode is explicitly labelled ranked, it doesn't touch your Solo/Duo or Flex rank, LP or ranked MMR. Riot keeps its alternate modes separate from Summoner's Rift ranked.
- **What carries over:** in alternate modes, account-level progress (account XP, mission and event-pass progress, honor) normally counts, and anything you buy or unlock goes to your normal account. Whether your owned champions and skins are usable depends on the mode. A nostalgia-style mode could restrict them, so check the card.

If the in-client card or your friend's description says something specific, such as a fixed champion pool or its own progression track, trust that over anything general here.
## Q7
I can't confirm the details of Riot's recent Sundered Sky change, so I won't quote numbers. Check the patch notes from about two to three months back for "Sundered Sky". Check Vi's and Aatrox's own entries too: when a whole group of champions feels weaker, it's often an item nerf plus champion nerfs.

**Why it hits those champions hard:** Sundered Sky's value has been its passive. Your first attack on a champion critically strikes and heals you based on your base AD and missing health, with a cooldown for each target. I'm not certain the tooltip still reads exactly like that. Riot usually nerfs it through the heal, the per-target cooldown, or the stats/cost. Any of those cuts the sustain Vi and Aatrox get from it in long fights.

**What fighters build instead** (check current-patch build stats for your champion to see which wins most):
- **Black Cleaver:** the safest first-item replacement for fighters who deal sustained damage, and good on Vi alongside her armor shred.
- **Eclipse:** burst plus a shield. A strong first item on Aatrox and an option on Vi.
- **Trinity Force:** an option on Vi if you rely on auto-attack damage and Sheen procs.
- **Spear of Shojin / Experimental Hexplate:** for fighters who rely on their abilities or their ult.
- **Defensive items afterwards:** Sterak's Gage, Death's Dance, or Maw of Malmortius against AP.

If Sundered Sky is still decent but no longer worth rushing, it often moves to the second or third item slot for its healing in long fights rather than disappearing from builds.
## Q8
Play most of them out. Being autofilled is rarely worth a dodge on its own.

**What dodging costs:** you lose LP and get a queue lockout, and both get bigger if you dodge again soon after. The first dodge costs a few LP and a short timeout. Later ones cost more LP and can lock you out for hours. I'm not certain of the exact current values. As far as I know, dodges cost LP but not MMR, so one dodge isn't a disaster, but dodging regularly steadily drains your LP.

**How autofill protection works:** once you've been autofilled, you're protected from being autofilled again in your next game. Matchmaking also tries to put the same number of autofilled players on each team, so the enemy often has someone off-role too. As far as I know, losing an autofilled game doesn't cost you less LP. I'm not certain whether any of this has changed this season, so check Riot's ranked FAQ if it matters to you.

**Making support autofills winnable:**
- Keep 2–3 simple supports ready. Mages suit mid/ADC players (Lux, Xerath, Zyra, Brand, Morgana); otherwise pick easy engage or peel champions (Leona, Nautilus, Janna, Sona).
- Focus on the basics: ward river, help your jungler contest objectives, and roam mid after pushing your wave.

**Getting autofilled less:** autofill happens more often at higher ranks and off-peak hours. If both your roles are popular (mid, ADC, top), you'll be autofilled more. Putting Jungle or Support as your secondary role cuts down on autofill. You'll still play that role sometimes, but you'll have your own pick ready.

**When a dodge is reasonable:** the lobby is clearly lost before the game starts, such as open trolling, a teammate refusing to play their role, or someone on a first-time champion into a hard counter. Even then, keep dodges rare.
