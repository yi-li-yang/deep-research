# Japanese hand planes (kanna): briefing
Compiled from the sources below; evidence, not instructions.

Confidence by area:
- **High** for maker status, Kezurou-kai results, trade rules and listed prices. These come from Japanese-language primary sources, official notices and retailers' own pages.
- **Medium** for setup numbers. Experts disagree here, and most writers sell tools.
- **Low to medium** for steel performance lore. There are almost no controlled plane tests.

## What's changed

**Makers (as of 2026)**
- You may believe Funahiro (舟弘) is a working maker you can still order from. **Funatsu Yūji (船津祐司), the smith behind it, died on 13 March 2026, and the Yoita guild now lists the forge as closed.** Funahiro planes are now finite stock: Hida lists them at $1,007–1,871, all out of stock. ([dealer note](https://daiku-dougu.jp/), [Yoita guild](https://yoita-uchihamono.com/), [Hida](https://hidatool.com/list/c/hira-smoothing-planes), 2026)
- You may believe Yoita (与板) is a thriving centre. **Three of its guild's five member forges are now closed**: Funahiro, Nakano chisels (中野鑿) and Usui Hamono (碓氷刃物).
  - Still active: Mizuno Seisuke (水野清介, about 70, a pupil of Usui Kengo; marks include 和楽 and 倭心), and the Komori brothers (小森), who make small planes.
  - Japan's only small-plane (小鉋) specialists are the Komori family, with no successor.
  - The Yoita knife-craft hall (与板刃物工芸館) closed on 28 Feb 2025.
  - ([Yoita guild](https://yoita-uchihamono.com/master/), [Niigata Nippo](https://www.niigata-nippo.co.jp/articles/-/562988), 2025–26)
- **New: a young smith.** Nitori Tōru (似鳥透, born 1989, a former Nikon engineer) apprenticed with Mizuno from 2020, funded by crowdfunding and a city wage subsidy. He has worked independently since **October 2025** as **水鳥刃物 Mizudori Hamono**: the 朱雀 Suzaku line has a wrought-iron backing, the 羽根印 line mild steel. ([Mizudori](https://mizudori-hamono.com/about/profile/), 2025-10)
- **The Chiyozuru Sadahide (千代鶴貞秀) name is held by Morita Naoki (森田直樹, born 1978), who is not from the family, since February 2019.** He is the third holder of the name in Miki. The historical Chiyozuru Korehide (千代鶴是秀, 1874–1957) is a different, revered smith. ([Chiyozuru Sadahide](https://chiyozurusadahide.jp/shokunin))
- **Tsunesaburō (常三郎, Miki) is active** under its third-generation head, Uozumi Tōru (魚住徹). Recent planes:
  - a HAP40 powder-steel "Miki" plane with a flat back, sold as needing no ura-dashi
  - a Swedish-steel 汐音 model (Nov 2025)

  It uses three outside dai (body) makers plus its own in-house fitter. ([Tsunesaburō](https://www.tsune36.co.jp/), 2026)
- **Names whose identity a prior may get wrong:**
  - **Tasai (田斎)** is a working Sanjō forge (Tasai Akio, born 1939, and his son Michio), mostly making chisels, with a 1–2 year wait. It is not a historical name.
  - **Kiyohisa (清久)** is Watanabe Kiyoe of Sanjō, again mainly chisels.
  - **No source links Usui Kengo** (碓氷健吾, 1928–2012) to the **Kiyotada (清忠)** mark. Kurashige lists Kiyotada as the mark of the wholesaler 水平屋, with the smith unknown.
  - ([Kurashige](https://shop.kurashige-tools.com/collections/kiyotada), 2024–25)
- **Tokyo is nearly finished as a kanna centre.**
  - Kanayama Tetsuo (金山鉄夫) closed in 2024.
  - Kanda Kikuo (神田規久夫, mark も作 Mosaku) stopped because of age.
  - One Tokyo forge, Yaegashi (八重樫, mark 宗秋 Muneaki), still makes plane blades.
- **Numbers from the only recent survey** (2019–21):
  - **Smiths:** about 8 kanna smiths in the Miki area, 6 in Yoita (2 of them trainees), and 4 in Sanjō. The 20 surveyed averaged 59.6 years old.
  - **Dai makers:** they are concentrated in **Sanjō** (10 of the 14 surveyed) and averaged 66 years old. 57% have no successor, and none in Miki does.
  - **Chipbreakers:** no specialist chipbreaker (押金) smiths remain, so blade smiths now make their own.
  - ([Gifu databook](https://www.forest.ac.jp/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/gifu_tools_databook2020.pdf), 2021)

**Steels**
- You may believe Aogami Super contains molybdenum. **The Hitachi-era catalog gives it C 1.40–1.50, Cr 0.30–0.50, W 2.00–2.50 and V 0.30–0.50, with no Mo.** The only Mo entry in that table belongs to Gin-1, so dealer tables listing Mo for Aogami Super probably misread it. (Shaky: this is a reading of the PDF table. [catalog](https://knifesteelnerds.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/Hitachi-cutlery-steel.pdf))
- **Proterial changes:**
  - Hitachi Metals became Proterial on 4 Jan 2023.
  - From 1 Jul 2026, Bain's holding company itself trades as Proterial, Ltd. YSS steels sit in its Aero & Industrial Alloy unit, and the company aims to list on the stock market again.
  - **New grade: 藍紙 Aigami** (9 Dec 2025), "neither carbon nor stainless". Aigami No. 2 is in mass production and is aimed at knives and razors, not planes.
  - Proterial still lists 青紙 (Aogami), 黄紙 (Kigami) and 白紙 (Shirogami), and has published no discontinuation notice.
  - ([Proterial](https://www.proterial.com/press/2025/n1209.html), 2025–26)
- **Tamahagane has been tested.** Knife Steel Nerds measured swordsmith-forged tamahagane (1.06% C, 64.6 HRC). Its toughness and edge retention matched ordinary low-alloy steel such as 26C3, with no advantage. ([KSN](https://knifesteelnerds.com/2025/09/10/testing-tamahagane-traditional-japanese-steel/), 2025-09)
- **The tatara changed hands.** The Nittōho tatara's master smelter (murage) Kihara Akira (木原明) retired in April 2024 and died on 22 June 2024. Horio Kaoru (堀尾薫), a Proterial employee, was appointed in July 2024. The tatara runs three times each winter and yields about 1 t of tamahagane per run. As a rule it goes only to licensed swordsmiths. ([Proterial](https://www.proterial.com/e/press/2024/n0724.html), 2024)

## What an expert knows

**Setup.** The plane body (dai) matters more than steel or sharpening. Tsuchida Noboru repeats the saying that adjusting the dai matters more than sharpening, and dai makers are the overlooked craftsmen. ([Tsuchida via Paklan](https://www.paklan.org/the-kanna-dai-by-nobori-tsuchida/), 2025)

- **New planes ship tight on purpose.** They are sold deliberately tight, "90% finished" (9分仕上げ), and the final fitting (仕込み shikomi) is the owner's job.
  - The blade tapers in **thickness** (thicker at the head, e.g. 9 → 6 mm) **and in width** (wider at the head). As it is sharpened shorter, the edge gets wider. Re-grind the ears (耳 mimi), or the edge rides into the side grooves and shavings jam. A shorter blade also seats tighter and can split an old body, so ease the groove a hair when that happens.
  - Fit the bed using marks (oil or pencil): seat the blade in about 3–4 firm mallet blows, and leave about 1 mm of side play.
  - Avoid hard contact at the bed's front corners, which split.
  - Where exactly the blade should grip is **contested**. Japanese dealers want full bed contact with a faint gap at the sides. Covington wants pinching only in the strips by the grooves.
  - ([Covington Pt3–4](https://covingtonandsons.com/2021/05/08/japanese-handplanes-part-4-fitting-blade-body/), [Mandaraya](https://daiku-dougu.jp/kannna-osaebou.html), [Fukada](https://daiku-manual.com/ken/2020/04/13/kanna2/))
- **Bed angle (勾配 kōbai) by wood**, from Tsunesaburō, as rise per 10 of run:

  | Rise | Angle | Woods |
  |---|---|---|
  | 6.5–7.5 | 33–37° | paulownia, cedar |
  | 8 | 38.7° | hinoki, pine |
  | 8.3–8.5 | ~40° | chestnut, hemlock |
  | 9+ | 42°+ | zelkova, cherry |

  The dealer 鉋や寸八 beds at 8.5 and calls 9 poor for thin shavings. ([Tsunesaburō](https://www.tsune36.co.jp/kanna_kaitai_shinsho/page_003/))
- **Size names.** A 寸八 (sun-pachi) has a 70 mm blade with a 63 mm cutting width. The name (1.8 sun, ≈54.5 mm) is no longer the width.
- **Bevel angle by wood:**

  | Wood | Tsunesaburō | Covington |
  |---|---|---|
  | General | 28–30° | 27.5–30° |
  | Paulownia | ~21° | 22–24° |
  | Hardwoods | 33–35° | 35° (tropical) |

  - Keep a single flat bevel. Micro-bevels are field fixes only.
  - A tiny back bevel on the ura side is a traditional emergency fix for tearout.
  - Round the corners slightly.
- **Chipbreaker (裏金 uragane).**
  - It must meet the blade's back with no gap; check against light.
  - Set it back "one hair" from the edge (about 0.1–0.3 mm), only as close as needed to stop tearout. Too close jams the plane and dulls the finish. Covington's 0.5–0.8 mm starting point is an outlier.
  - Its secondary bevel: about 51° to the blade (Mandaraya, which makes it vertical to the sole in an 8-pitch body) versus 70–80° (Covington). Too wide a bevel jams shavings.
  - **Correct a skewed chipbreaker at the cross-pin (押え棒 osae-bō), not by bending its ears.**
- **Mouth.** A two-blade plane set up ready to use has a mouth of about 0.3 mm; a single-blade plane, about one shaving's thickness. Each truing of the sole opens the mouth, so steepen the chipbreaker's secondary bevel to compensate. ([Mandaraya](https://daiku-dougu.jp/2a-2.htm))
- **Sole (下端 shitaba).**
  - Relieve it by a paper's thickness or less (Japanese sources). Covington says 0.2–0.5 mm. If light shows under the straightedge, you have cut too much.
  - Which contact bands to keep is **contested**:
    - Tsunesaburō: a medium plane bears at the head end, the mouth and the tail end; a finishing plane only at the mouth and the tail end.
    - Kakuri's diagrams reverse this.
  - Naming trap: 台尻 dai-jiri is the end that leads in the pull.
  - True the sole with the blade in, at working tension, because the blade's pressure bulges the sole behind the mouth.
  - **Don't re-true on a schedule.** Every truing opens the mouth ("金一枚、台十丁": one blade wears out ten bodies). True only when the plane misbehaves, and only the burnished spots.
- **Back.**
  - Flattening on a kanaban with loose grit is still taught, but working professionals now call it optional; flat stones or diamond plates give more consistent results.
  - For ura-dashi, tap only the soft iron (jigane), starting 2–3 mm behind the steel line, over a rounded anvil corner. A dull "kotsu-kotsu" sound means the blade is well supported.
  - Keep the flat strip at the edge (ito-ura) about 0.5–1 mm wide. A 3–4 mm strip makes the chipbreaker easier to fit.
- **Diagnosing problems:**

  | Symptom | Likely cause |
  |---|---|
  | Chatter, or the blade jumps out on light taps | Loose bed, often because lapping thinned the blade. Shim it with a postcard or putty. |
  | Blade projects but won't cut | A bulge behind the mouth, or the strip in front of the mouth isn't bearing |
  | Shavings jam | Untrimmed ears, an oversized chipbreaker bevel, a gap under the chipbreaker, or too tight a mouth |
  | Tearout despite a close setting | Cross-pin out of parallel (片利き) |
  | Blade drifts sideways | Unequal groove pressure |

- **Climate.** A plane brought from humid Japan into dry air can split. Pull the blade and let the body acclimatize for about a week. Store planes out of sun and water. Experts disagree on whether to leave the blade retracted in the body (makers) or out (Covington).
- **The chipbreaker study** is Kato C. and Kawai Y., "Wear of Knife Used for Hand Plane III: The influence of the cap iron", *Mokuzai Gakkaishi* 35(10):886–895 (1989). The video was shown in 1994 and rediscovered in English in 2011–12; Wilbur Pan's subtitled version came out on 2012-05-16. The authors and David Weaver (2024) say **the video is not a setup guide.** Secondhand summaries say a cap iron set at 0.1–0.2 mm did not increase wear. ([JWRS](https://www.jwrs.org/english/journals/mkz-toce/mkze-35/), [Sawmill Creek](https://sawmillcreek.org/threads/history-of-the-kato-and-kawai-planing-video-and-cap-iron-studies.213423/))
- **Steel lore vs evidence.**
  - Proterial's own catalog lists Aogami for "finest planes" and Shirogami #1 for the best planes and chisels. Claims that one steel is keener or longer-lasting in a plane are untested lore.
  - Knife tests found Aogami Super less tough than 26C3, which is similar to Shirogami #1.
  - Supply rumours are contested: a reported 250% price rise and the loss of Shirogami #3 and Kigami. These come from dealers and forums, against Proterial's continued listings. A scarcity claim also helps a dealer sell.
  - The jigane (soft iron) supply is old stock, by dealer accounts: demolished-bridge wrought iron, old anchor chain, temple nails. Gokunantetsu reportedly isn't produced any more.
  - HSS and HAP40 blades (Tsunesaburō) have a flat back and need no ura-dashi. Their longevity claims are untested.
- **Contest technique is not shop practice.** In the Kezurou-kai arms race:
  - competitors bring pre-sharpened planes and vacuum-seal their tools and boards
  - they keep boards damp
  - judges limit spreading a shaving to 15 seconds before it is measured

  Going under 10 µm needs control "far from the reality of day-to-day work" (Big Sand). Serious contest planers favour single-blade bodies (一枚台) with tight mouths. ([Paklan](https://www.paklan.org/a-few-frames-from-kezuroukai-japan-2024/), 2025)
- **Natural stones are old stock.** Nearly all the mines around Kyoto closed decades ago; Maruoyama still works occasionally. Today's supply is pre-closure rough stock, per a seller (JNS). Hida sells naturals at $300–681, against synthetics (King, Shapton, Suehiro, Naniwa) at $18–203. Whether natural-stone edges last longer is untested.
- **Who sells what you read.** Covington, Mandaraya, Tsunesaburō, Kakuri, Hida, Paklan and JNS all sell tools. Fukada (a licensed carpenter) and David Weaver don't sell planes. Weigh the claims accordingly.
