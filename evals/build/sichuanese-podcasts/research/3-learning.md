### Phonology: what differs from Putonghua (en.wikipedia, quoted via digest)
- "Four Sichuanese initial consonants do not exist in Beijing: [z], [v], [ŋ] and [nʲ]. On the other hand, five initials in Beijing do not exist in Sichuanese: [tʂ], [tʂʰ], [ʂ], [ʐ] and [l]." (the [l] claim is odd: other sources say n/l are in free variation in Chengdu — see Wiktionary below; FLAG). Finals: Sichuanese has [ɛ], [iai], [uɛ], [yo] that Beijing lacks; Beijing [ɤ], [iŋ], [əŋ] absent in Sichuanese (i.e. -ing/-eng merge into -in/-en). — en.wikipedia Sichuanese dialect — accessed 2026-10-07
- zh.wikipedia examples: [z] 人 [zən2], 如 [zu2]; [v] 五 [vu3]; [ŋ] 我 [ŋo3]; [n̠ʲ] 你 [n̠ʲi3]. 绝大多数地区无 tʂ 组卷舌声母. 成都话 36 类韵母. — zh.wikipedia — accessed 2026-10-07
- Wiktionary: initial n is "free variation between [n], [l], [l̃]"; ng and ny initials "may be omitted in certain words". — Wiktionary:About Chinese/Sichuanese — accessed 2026-10-07

### zh.wikipedia 成都话 (digest; page 128k chars, read first 100k) — accessed 2026-10-07
- Tones (Chengdu): 阴平 45, 阳平 21, 上声 42, 去声 13; 入声普遍归阳平 (example given: 七 sounds like 齐). NOTE this page says 去声 13 whereas 四川话 page and en.wikipedia say 213, and 上声 42 vs 53 elsewhere. Notation noise across sources, same shape.
- Sound features listed: no retroflex/dental split (祖=主, 色=舌); n/l merge before open vowels (纳=辣, 南=蓝); 疑影母开口 read ŋ- (我 ŋo, 硬 ŋən); fu/hu not distinguished before single-vowel u (呼=肤); in/ing and en/eng merge (因=英, 森=僧) — the page quotes "不区分[ən]和[ɤŋ]，[in]和[iŋ]这两对韵母"; 尖团 merged (绝=决, 千=谦).
- Shift note: "受普通话的影响，成都话相对稳定的状态被打破，语音开始经历一个明显变化的时期"; younger speakers converge to Putonghua first in high-frequency formal/book-register words, while colloquial words resist.
- References cited: 杨时逢 (1984) 《四川方言调查报告》 (史语所专刊之82); 张绍诚 (2005) 《巴蜀方言浅说》 (巴蜀书社); 周及徐 (2001) 《20世纪成都话音变研究》 (四川师范大学学报 28(4)); 夏中易 (2002) 《近四十年成都话语音变动现象考论》 (成都大学学报 2002(4)).

### zh.wikipedia 重庆话 (digest; 146k chars, read first 100k) — accessed 2026-10-07
- Tones: 阴平 55, 阳平 21, 上声 42, 去声 214; 古入声归阳平 (same as other 川黔片).
- Stated key phonological differences from Chengdu: (a) Chongqing has no /ȵ/ initial: Chengdu words with /ȵ/ (研, 宜) go to /l/ in Chongqing, so "l与n洪混细也混" (n/l merge even before i: 你 and 里 collide); (b) 川东 speech incl. Chongqing has an extra final /yu/ where Chengdu has /yo/ in some 通摄入声 words.
- Quote from page (翟时雨 source): the two are "在听感上拥有较大差异" yet "音韵上除少数例外字外，仅有两点主要区别" (listeners hear a sizeable difference; phonologically, bar a few exception words, there are only two main differences).
- Scope: "学术上所指的重庆话，仅仅只是重庆市主城区的语言，使用人口约占重庆全市人口的五分之一" — "Chongqing speech" in academic usage = main urban area only, about one fifth of the municipality's population.
- Classified as 西南官话 > 川黔片 > 成渝小片.
- References: 翟时雨 (1999) 《成都、重庆话在四川方言分区中的地位》 (西南师范大学学报(哲社版) 1999(2)); 蓝勇 (1997) 《西南历史文化地理》; 钟维克 (2005) 《重庆方言音系研究》 (重庆社会科学 2005(6)).

## Batch 4 (en.wikipedia Chengdu-Chongqing page, corpus, young-speaker data, practitioner pieces)

### en.wikipedia "Chengdu-Chongqing dialect" (digest) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu-Chongqing_dialect — accessed 2026-10-07
- Defines Cheng-Yu as "the most widely used branch of Southwestern Mandarin, with about 90 million speakers"; formed after Ming-Qing migration, "greatly influenced by the Chinese varieties of Mandarin the immigrants spoke from Hubei, as well as Xiang and Gan"; keeps fewer Ba-Shu features than other Sichuanese dialects (Minjiang etc.). (Sichuanese overall is given as ~120 million on the other page — don't mix the two numbers.)
- Tone table here: Chengdu 55 / 31 / 53 / 213; Chongqing 55 / 21 / 42 / 214 (a FOURTH set of Chengdu values: 阳平 31 not 21). Wikipedia's own pages disagree with each other; Wiktionary's Chengdu set is 55/21/53/213.
- Tone sandhi (cites Tan Lunhua, 1998): in compounds the second syllable often goes to tone 1 after tone 2 or 4 (爸爸 pa2pa1, 婆婆 po2po1); erhua after tone 2 (娃儿 ua2ɚ1); function words 去 头 面 上 go to tone 1. Relevant for listeners: unstressed second syllables sound high/short.
- Cui Rongchang (1996) cited for "only 47.8% similar vocabulary" to northern Mandarin. FLAG: single old figure, method unknown.
- Zhai Shiyu (1996) cited: many Chongqing residents "cannot speak the local dialect but can usually understand it, and attain fluency in it after spending a few months or years in the area"; Cui & Song (1999): literary readings are becoming dominant. FLAG: dated.
- Literary vs colloquial readings (from 《成都语音的初步研究》 1958): 严 colloquial ŋan2 / literary nʲian2; 下 colloquial xa4 / literary ɕia4 — i.e. the same character can have a "dialect" and a "book" reading, and younger speakers drift to the book reading.
- No grammar/particles section on this page either.

- Ximalaya Q&A pages (m.ximalaya.com/ask/…) offer crowd-sourced "how to learn 四川话 fast" tips, e.g. a rule of thumb that Putonghua tone 1 stays, tone 2 -> low tone, tone 3 -> falling tone, tone 4 -> rising tone, and zh/ch/sh -> z/c/s. These match the published tone values (阳平 21 low-falling, 上声 53/42 high-falling, 去声 13/213 rising) but break on historical entering-tone words, which all go to 阳平.
